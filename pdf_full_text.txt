GENERAL INTRODUCTION
Decision making is one of the most critical processes for any enterprise, entrepreneurs make
daily decisions about almost everything. Some of those are rather simple with minimum eﬀects
on the company but others could inﬂuence the overall business processes, those particular ones
need enough informations to make sure that the right decision is being taken, but in reality,
this is not always the case.
In a survey done by "BI-survey.com" 50% of entrepreneurs claim that they make decisions
without the necessary informations, 40% claim that the quality of data they get is not good
enough and other 32% claim that the relevant data is too expensive to get. [1]
At a time when competitive intensity is raging, manufacturers and distributors are competing
to gain new shares in local markets. In order to ensure a good share several companies employ
agents on the ground which have a main task to collect data about the market, tailor local
marketing campaigns based on area trends and ensure product appearance and supply in various
stores throughout their designated geographic area and that’s exactly the job of a merchandiser.
Despite how crucial the merchandiser’s job is in collecting data and ensuring local operations
including sales, supply and marketing most of the work is currently done manually, decreasing
the eﬃciency of the merchandiser and the quality of collected data.

This end of studies project aims to solve those issues facing the merchandising team by
conceiving and developing a web and mobile application called "Merchandising Team".
To attain our goal we divided this internship report into 3 major parts:
• First chapter "State of the art» this chapter focuses on the analysis of the existing solu-

• Second chapter "Detailed Conception of the application" in which a detailed conception
is given, a major step before implementing our solution.
• Third chapter "Realisation of the project», in this chapter a justiﬁcation about the choice
of technologies used is provided alongside with screenshots of our application.
Finally, we conclude our work with a general conclusion in which we discuss the future
perspectives of our solution and the potential changes we intend to implement in order to
further improve it and make it more eﬃcient for the merchandisers.

tracking the shelf’s’state.
1.2.2
Suggested solution
We suggest a multiplatform based solution (web application for the administrator and a
mobile application for merchandisers) which could digitalise the merchandiser’s job.
This solution should support multiple events, a dashboard with statistics for the administra-
tor, an ergonomic, and simple to use interface and collects data about competitors and their
activities which could be used to plan marketing campaigns.
Our application should contain a map to guide merchandisers to their target supermarkets
and allow them to communicate with each other using a messaging service, moreover they
should be able to send claims and leave requests.

Requirement speciﬁcation
A software requirements speciﬁcation is the description of the system to be developed and
its functional and non-functional requirements.it forms the basis for any agreement between

1.3.1
Identifying major actors in the system
The administrator is a major actor in our solution, its main duties consists of the management
of products, competitor, leave request, claims and creating the planning for each merchandiser.
The second actor is the merchandiser whose main duties are to follow the planning and create
reports for each visited GMS.
4State of the Art

1.3.2
Functional requirements
Our solution should contain the following requirements:
the administrator:
• The administrator is the actor tasked with creating user accounts, updating those
accounts when needed and deleting them.
• The administrator has the possibility of creating, updating and deleting any target
supermarket in the system and have full access to its data.
• Using his interface, the administrator is able to add new products, updating and
deleting them.
• The administrator manages daily and monthly schedule of all merchandisers.
• The administrator receive notiﬁcations regarding the merchandiser’s actions, reports
or messaging service notiﬁcations.
• All reclamations are reported to the administrator who could list them.
• The administrator could use the messaging service to communicate with his fellow
merchandisers.
• The administrator receives leave requests from merchandisers and has the possibility
to either accept or reject them.
the merchandiser:
• The merchandiser could report a reclamation to the administrator.
• Merchandisers have the ability to send leave requests.
• Merchandisers could do the referencing of the company products inside a particular
supermarket.
• Merchandisers could use the messaging service to communicate with other merchan-
disers or the administrator.
• Merchandisers could follow a daily planning and see their monthly planning.
• Merchandisers should send a report for every visit to supermarket they made and
they could see the target supermarkets on a map.

1.3.3
Non-functional requirements
Non-functional requirements deﬁne system attributes which are mostly hidden from the end
user.
Our application should obey to the following rules:
• Ergonomics: this solution should be user friendly with an attractive design and we will
ensure that by using some famous React UI frameworks such as “Material UI”.
• Maintainability:while coding we try as much as possible to stick to the best practises
of software development and design patterns to ensure an easy maintainability of our
product.
• Security:By logging to the server, the user of the application (both administrator and
merchandiser) acquire a JWT token that has to be send with every call to the backend
to ensure his identity.
• Interoperability: we use technologies that ensure our product could be used on various
platforms and operation systems (React Native for mobile, spring boot for backend and
react for web from a single codebase ).

2.1
Design methodology
To design our system we choose to use UML (Uniﬁed modeling Language) as an object ori-
ented modeling language.
UML is a general purpose object oriented modeling language which uses various types of dia-
grams to provide a standard graphical language to visualize the design of a system by specifying
its structure, behavior and interactions.
UML helps to simplify complex software design by reducing lots of words of explanation to a
few graphical, more understandable diagrams.

Sequence diagram
A sequence diagram simply depicts interaction between objects in a sequential order (the

2.4.1
Sequence diagram of “Login”
The following sequence diagram illustrates the sequence of interactions between objects in

Figure 2.17: Sequence diagram of “Login”
In order to login, the user has to enter his username (which is the email in our case) and his
password, a ﬁrst validation in the frontend takes place to verify the format of the email.
18conceptual study
28
If the ﬁrst validation is veriﬁed a post request is sent to the authentication ﬁlter of spring
security which sends the request to user model in order to verify the existence of that particular
user.
If a user with the speciﬁed email and password do not exists an error message is returned from
the user model to the authentication ﬁlter which returns an error response to the view.
In case the speciﬁed user exists, user details are returned from the model to the authentication
ﬁlter which creates a JWT token and returns it to the view alongside the user’s data to signify
the operation’s success and the user is taken to the home page.

Figure 2.18: Sequence diagram of “create user”

To create a new user, the admin enters various user details in a form which is veriﬁed in

missing.
If all needed ﬁelds are ﬁlled a post request is send to the controller which created a request for
the user model. The user model validate the existence of any other identical email (emails are
our unique identiﬁer in this application). In case another identical email exists in our database
the model informs the controller which returns an error response in an http response to the
view indicating that the typed email already exists.
In case no other identical email is found in our database, the new user is added and user details
are returned to the controller which returns an Http response indicating the success of the
operation to the user.

N-tier architecture or multi-layer architecture is client-server architecture that separates re-
sponsibilities between the layers. Usually we have 3 tiers which handle presentation, business
logic, and data respectfully.

32
This separation of concerns allows the developer to create scalable and highly maintainable
software.
• Presentation Tier :
this is the client or the user interface , it is the layer that is
presented to the end user.
•
Business Logic Tier : where we ﬁnd most of the logic for a system, usually as a
middleman between what the user sees and the stored data.
•
Data Tier : the place where all the data used by the application is stored (commonly
a database)

MVC pattern
Model-View-Controller or MVC for short is a software design pattern widely used for devel-
oping user interfaces. This pattern facilitate the development of systems where we need the
separate the logic of the way data is presented to and accepted from the user. MVC is very
popular in the world of web development and therefore for each popular language there exists
a MVC framework that helps in the implementation of the pattern.

•
View : In accordance with the name this is what the user sees, in the case of a mobile
app that refers to the diﬀerent screens.
•
Model : A dynamic data structure that represents stored data in the app (for example
each model could represent a database table)
•
Controller : The main piece of the system, generally handles most of the logic and
ensures communication between the view and the model.

CONCLUSION& PERSPECTIVE

to create a solution named “merchandising team », a web and mobile application to digitalize

send claims and leave requests and reference the products in a given supermarket. As for the

monitoring all of the merchandiser team’s activities
